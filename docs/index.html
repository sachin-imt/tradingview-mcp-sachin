The reaction, not the print, is the investment case. Revenue rose 85.5% year over year to $29.6B, adjusted EPS rose 96.2% to $3.32, and operating margin expanded 237bps to 67.9%. The stock rallied 3.4% to $379.69 — then surrendered the entire move, trading down to $361.20. AJ reads this as a market treating earnings compounding at ~100% as "borrowed rather than earned."

A multiple dislocation, not an estimate dislocation. This is the analytical hinge: AJ's FY2027 estimate of $18.48 sits 6% below the consensus average of $19.57. He is not asking anyone to accept a more aggressive forecast than the sell side already carries — the street is higher, and the equity still trades at 20.9x. "The market is not disputing the estimates. It is declining to capitalise them."

The gross-margin bear case is the wrong debate. Gross margin fell 336bps to 75.0%, which is what the market reacted to. But operating margin expanded in the same period — the compression is mix (custom accelerator silicon vs 90%+ margin software), not price. Broadcom added ~$13.6B of quarterly revenue while adding almost nothing to its cost base; incremental operating margin was ~71%, higher than reported gross margin.

The inversion frames the downside. For a buyer at $361 to make nothing over twelve months, the forward multiple must compress from 20.9x to 13.5x — a level unseen since before AI revenue was material. Implied PEG at that point: 0.24x. "The bull case requires the market to pay what it has recently paid. The bear case requires the market to pay what it paid before the growth existed."

Track record earns the forward numbers a hearing. Three consecutive quarters of materially better accuracy than consensus — 0.1% error last quarter (consensus 2.3%), 0.10% the quarter before (consensus 1.64%), 1.0% before that (consensus 2.4%). AJ is careful to note this validates near-term order-flow modelling, not a multi-year forecast.

  • Key risk — concentration: FY28 estimate embeds ~$233B of AI semi revenue vs ~$115B in FY27, a doubling off an enormous base. The single most demanding assumption in the work.
  • Key risk — persistent derating: the market may keep applying a low-teens terminal multiple indefinitely. "This is the risk that most deserves respect, because it is the one the price action is currently signalling."
  • Key risk — customer concentration: few very large custom-accelerator customers; the weekly names Anthropic specifically as the concentration the market worries about.
  • Catalyst: Q4 FY2026 print in December — first substantive framing of the FY2027 AI revenue base.

AJ's PEG Definition — Growth One Year Out, Not Next Year

Sep 3 · X

AJ spelled out the methodology behind the Cockpit's y-axis for the first time. His growth term is NTM+1 earnings ÷ NTM earnings — not this year's growth, but the growth the market expects over the twelve months starting a year from now.

The reasoning: "Investors should not focus on the next twelve months. Investors should focus on what investors believe the next twelve months in twelve months can deliver." Forward P/E already captures NTM; pairing it with NTM growth would double-count the same window. Rolling NTM intervals are used throughout rather than calendar years, since what matters is a consistent 1-year period, not January-to-December.

This matters for reading the Cockpit: a low PEG on AJ's definition means the market is cheaply pricing growth that is still two years out — which is exactly the AVGO argument below.

August CPI (Sep 11) — Oil Base Effect Sets Up a Favorable Print

Sep 2 · X

The oil scare is misread. "Oil is freaking investors out" — but the accurate framing is that oil is worrying investors that other investors would be selling. August 2026 delivered oil at $83, comparable to June's $82. In June, oil contributed 86bps (0.86pp) to headline CPI.

The base effect is what matters. June 2025 average oil was $67; August 2025 was $64 — $3 lower. So August 2026 compares against a lower 2025 bar than the June 2026 print did. The August 2026 oil contribution to CPI will therefore likely exceed June's 86bps.

Why that is bullish, not bearish. July CPI printed 3.4%. Consensus (incomplete) points to another 3.4% for August. If headline holds flat while the energy contribution rises, the arithmetic implies non-energy inflation drivers actually eased — a genuinely positive signal that the market is currently mispricing as an oil problem.

AJ's read: "I don't want to sound too bullish but there is a reasonably likely pathway for a favorable development next week." August CPI drops Friday, September 11. Separately, AJ reposted his May thesis: the administration stays focused on pulling pump prices down ahead of the Midterms.
